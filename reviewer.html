ENVIRONMENTAL SCIENCE REVIEWER

200 KEY TERMS • STUDENT-FRIENDLY EDITION

How to use this reviewer

Read each term and its definition. Pay attention to key words, scientific formulas, percentages, and acronyms.

1

Environment

Everything around us, including all living (biotic) and non-living (abiotic) components.

2

Ecosystem

A system where living organisms interact with one another and with their physical surroundings.

3

Ecology

The study of the relationships between living organisms and their environment.

4

Ecologist

A scientist who studies ecosystems and the interactions between organisms and their surroundings.

5

Biotic Factors

The living components of an ecosystem (e.g., plants, animals, fungi, bacteria, humans).

6

Abiotic Factors

The non-living physical and chemical elements in an ecosystem (e.g., sunlight, water, air, soil, temperature, rocks).

7

Biosphere

The global ecological system integrating all living beings and their relationships across land, water, and air.

8

Biome

A large naturally occurring community of flora and fauna occupying a major habitat.

9

Habitat

The natural home or dwelling place of an organism that provides food, water, and shelter.

10

Species

A group of organisms commonly considered similar and, in many cases, capable of producing fertile offspring; this is one common species concept.

11

Population

A group of organisms belonging to the same species living in a specific area at the same time.

12

Community

Different populations of organisms living and interacting together in a shared area.

13

Biodiversity

The variety of life on Earth across genetic, species, and ecosystem levels.

14

30 by 30 Target

A target under the Kunming-Montreal Global Biodiversity Framework aiming to conserve at least 30% of Earth's terrestrial, inland water, coastal, and marine areas by 2030.

15

Endangered Species

A species of plant or animal at serious risk of extinction.

16

Extinction

The permanent disappearance of a species when no living member remains on Earth.

17

Invasive Species

A non-native organism that establishes and spreads in a new area and causes, or is likely to cause, harm to the environment, economy, or people.

18

Poaching

The illegal hunting, capturing, or killing of protected wild animals.

19

Overfishing

Catching fish at a rate faster than populations can naturally replenish.

20

Producers (Autotrophs)

Organisms that make their own food through processes like photosynthesis (e.g., plants, algae).

21

Photosynthesis

The chemical process where green plants use sunlight, carbon dioxide (CO₂), and water to create food and oxygen (O₂).

22

Chlorophyll

The green pigment in plant cells that absorbs light energy required for photosynthesis.

23

Consumers (Heterotrophs)

Organisms that obtain energy by consuming other living organisms.

24

Primary Consumer

An animal that feeds directly on producers (herbivore).

25

Secondary Consumer

An animal that eats primary consumers (carnivore or omnivore).

26

Tertiary Consumer

An organism that feeds on secondary consumers; it may be an apex predator, but not always.

27

Herbivore

An animal that mainly eats plants.

28

Carnivore

An animal that mainly eats other animals.

29

Omnivore

An animal that regularly consumes both plants and animals.

30

Decomposer

Organisms (e.g., bacteria, fungi) that break down dead plants, animals, and organic matter, returning nutrients to the soil.

31

Detritivore

An organism that physically consumes dead organic debris (detritus).

32

Scavenger

An animal that feeds on dead animal carcasses it did not kill itself.

33

Food Chain

A single linear sequence showing how energy passes from one organism to another.

34

Food Web

A complex network of interconnected and interdependent food chains in an ecosystem.

35

Trophic Level

Each hierarchical step or feeding level occupied by an organism in a food chain.

36

Ten Percent Law

The principle stating that only about 10% of energy is transferred to the next higher trophic level.

37

Biomass

The total mass of living organic matter within a given area or volume.

38

Cellular Respiration

The process by which cells break down food molecules to release usable energy (ATP); some forms require oxygen, while others do not.

39

Symbiosis

A close, long-term biological interaction between two different species.

40

Mutualism

A symbiotic relationship where both participating organisms benefit.

41

Commensalism

A symbiotic interaction where one organism benefits while the other is unaffected.

42

Parasitism

A symbiotic interaction where one organism (parasite) benefits at the expense of another (host).

43

Predation

An interaction where one organism (predator) hunts, kills, and feeds on another (prey).

44

Competition

The struggle between organisms for limited resources like food, water, light, and space.

45

Camouflage

An adaptation allowing an organism to blend into its environment to avoid detection.

46

Mimicry

An adaptation in which an organism resembles another organism or object; this resemblance may help it avoid predators or gain another advantage.

47

Ecological Succession

The gradual process by which ecosystem structure and species composition change over time.

48

Primary Succession

Ecological succession occurring on bare rock or land with no pre-existing soil.

49

Secondary Succession

Succession occurring in an area where a disturbance destroyed a community but left soil intact.

50

Natural Resource

Any material or substance obtained from nature that humans use.

51

Renewable Resource

A natural resource that is replenished by natural processes on a human timescale, although it may still be depleted locally if overused.

52

Non-Renewable Resource

A natural resource that is not replenished on a human timescale, or is used much faster than it can be replaced.

53

Fossil Fuels

Non-renewable energy sources formed from ancient organic matter, such as coal, petroleum (oil), and natural gas.

54

Solar Energy

Renewable energy obtained from sunlight using solar panels.

55

Wind Energy

Energy captured by using moving air currents to turn wind turbines.

56

Hydroelectric Energy

Renewable electricity generated from moving or falling water.

57

Geothermal Energy

Heat energy generated and trapped deep inside the Earth.

58

Green Hydrogen

Hydrogen produced by splitting water through electrolysis using renewable electricity.

59

Soil

The outermost layer of Earth's crust composed of rock particles, mineral nutrients, organic matter, water, and air.

60

Humus

Dark, nutrient-rich organic matter created by the decay of plant and animal tissues in soil.

61

Topsoil

The fertile upper layer of soil containing high organic matter and essential plant nutrients.

62

Bedrock

The solid rock beneath soil or loose surface material; it may be exposed at Earth's surface.

63

Groundwater

Water located beneath Earth's surface stored in soil pores and rock formations.

64

Aquifer

An underground layer of permeable rock or sediment holding groundwater.

65

Watershed

An area of land where all precipitation drains into a common body of water.

66

Soil Erosion

The wearing away and displacement of topsoil or rock by agents like water, wind, ice, and gravity.

67

Deforestation

The clearance or removal of forest cover for non-forest land uses.

68

Reforestation

Restoring trees to land where forests were previously removed or degraded.

69

Afforestation

Establishing a forest on land that has not been recently forested; it should not replace naturally treeless ecosystems.

70

Contour Plowing

Plowing across sloping land along constant elevation lines to minimize water runoff and topsoil erosion.

71

Terracing

Cutting step-like flat areas into hillsides to reduce surface runoff and prevent erosion.

72

Regenerative Agriculture

An agricultural approach focused on improving soil health, water retention, and ecosystem function (e.g., cover crops, reduced tillage).

73

Crop Rotation

Growing different crops in succession on the same land to maintain soil fertility and break pest cycles.

74

Composting

The controlled decomposition of organic waste into compost, which can add organic matter and improve soil.

75

Water Cycle

The continuous movement of water through Earth's surface, underground, and atmosphere.

76

Evaporation

The change of liquid water into water vapor, usually after absorbing heat.

77

Transpiration

The process where water moves through plants and evaporates into the air through leaves.

78

Condensation

The cooling of water vapor in the air changing back into liquid droplets to form clouds.

79

Precipitation

Water falling from clouds to Earth in liquid or frozen forms (e.g., rain, snow, hail).

80

Collection

Water gathers in rivers, lakes, oceans, and underground aquifers as part of the water cycle.

81

Water Conservation

Practicing wise and minimal use of water resources to prevent waste.

82

Atmosphere

The protective envelope of gases surrounding Earth.

83

Troposphere

The lowest layer of Earth's atmosphere where weather occurs and humans live.

84

Stratosphere

The atmospheric layer above the troposphere that houses the protective ozone layer.

85

Ozone Layer

A region in the stratosphere that absorbs and filters out harmful solar ultraviolet (UV) radiation.

86

Weather

The short-term state of atmospheric conditions at a specific time and place.

87

Climate

The long-term patterns and average atmospheric conditions in a region over an extended period.

88

Barometer

An instrument used to measure atmospheric pressure.

89

Anemometer

An instrument used to measure wind speed.

90

Hygrometer

An instrument used to measure relative atmospheric humidity.

91

Wind Vane

A tool used to determine wind direction.

92

Thermometer

An instrument used to measure temperature.

93

Rain Gauge

An instrument used to collect and measure liquid precipitation over time.

94

Cirrus Clouds

High, thin, feathery clouds composed mainly of ice crystals.

95

Cumulus Clouds

Puffy clouds with fairly flat bases that often occur in fair weather but can grow into storm clouds.

96

Stratus Clouds

Low, flat, grey cloud sheets covering the sky like a uniform blanket.

97

Cumulonimbus Clouds

Towering clouds associated with thunderstorms, heavy rain, and severe weather.

98

Typhoon

A tropical cyclone in the western North Pacific with sustained winds that meet the typhoon-strength threshold.

99

Eye of a Typhoon

The calm, low-pressure central area of a tropical cyclone.

100

Eyewall

The ring of intense thunderstorms surrounding a tropical cyclone's eye, where the strongest winds are often found.

101

Storm Surge

An abnormal rise of coastal water generated by a severe storm's winds and low pressure.

102

Monsoon

A seasonal wind pattern bringing dramatic precipitation shifts (Habagat and Amihan).

103

El Niño

A climate pattern involving unusual warming in the tropical Pacific that can reduce rainfall and increase drought risk in parts of the Philippines; impacts vary by place and event.

104

Pollution

The contamination of the environment by harmful chemical, physical, or biological agents.

105

Pollutant

Any substance or form of energy that causes environmental contamination or ecological harm.

106

Air Pollution

Contamination of the atmosphere by vehicle exhaust, factory emissions, and fossil fuel burning.

107

Water Pollution

Contamination of water bodies by sewage, oil spills, toxic chemicals, and agricultural runoff.

108

Soil Pollution

Land degradation caused by improper waste disposal, industrial spills, and chemical pesticides.

109

Noise Pollution

Harmful, unwanted, or excessive sound from traffic, machinery, or construction.

110

Light Pollution

Excessive, misdirected artificial light at night that disrupts wildlife and hides stars.

111

Point Source Pollution

Contamination originating from a single identifiable, localized source (e.g., factory pipe).

112

Non-Point Source Pollution

Pollutants entering water bodies from widespread, diffuse land areas (e.g., agricultural runoff).

113

Smog

Air pollution involving a mixture of pollutants; some types include ground-level ozone formed when pollutants react in sunlight.

114

Acid Rain

Rainfall made abnormally acidic by atmospheric reactions with sulfur dioxide (SO₂) and nitrogen oxides (NO_x) from fossil fuel burning.

115

Eutrophication

Excess nutrients entering a water body can promote excessive algal growth and, in some conditions, reduce dissolved oxygen.

116

Algal Bloom

A rapid increase or accumulation of microscopic algae populations in aquatic ecosystems.

117

Red Tide

A common term for certain harmful algal blooms; the water may change color, but not all harmful blooms are red or caused by the same organisms.

118

Thermal Pollution

A human-caused change in water temperature, often from warm-water discharges, that can harm aquatic life and affect dissolved oxygen.

119

Plastic Pollution

Accumulation of non-biodegradable synthetic plastic items in land and marine environments.

120

Microplastics

Synthetic plastic particles smaller than 5 millimeters found widespread in oceans, air, and soil.

121

Bioaccumulation

The gradual accumulation of toxic chemicals inside a single organism over its lifetime.

122

Biomagnification

The increasing concentration of persistent toxins in organisms at higher trophic levels in a food chain.

123

3 Rs of Waste Management

Reduce, Reuse, Recycle—the familiar framework for minimizing waste.

124

Reduce

Decreasing the volume of resources used and waste produced at the source.

125

Reuse

Using an item multiple times before considering discarding it.

126

Recycle

Processing used waste materials to manufacture new products.

127

Circular Economy

An economic system that keeps products and materials in use through approaches such as Reduce → Reuse → Repair → Refurbish → Recycle.

128

Linear Economy

A resource-use pattern often described as take → make → use → throw away.

129

Solid Waste

Discarded solid or semi-solid materials from homes, businesses, institutions, and other activities.

130

Biodegradable Waste

Waste materials naturally decomposable by microorganisms (e.g., food scraps, leaves).

131

Non-Biodegradable Waste

Waste materials resistant to natural biological decomposition (e.g., plastics, metals).

132

Hazardous Waste

Toxic, reactive, or dangerous waste materials posing health and environmental threats.

133

Electronic Waste (E-waste)

Discarded electrical or electronic equipment (e.g., broken phones, computers) containing hazardous substances and valuable metals.

134

Landfill

A site engineered for disposing of solid waste by compaction and burial under soil layers.

135

Sanitary Landfill

A modern landfill lined with synthetic barriers and leachate collection systems to protect surrounding groundwater.

136

Leachate

Liquid that passes through waste and picks up dissolved or suspended substances; its composition and risk depend on the waste.

137

Incineration

High-temperature controlled combustion of solid waste materials.

138

Zero Waste

A sustainability goal focused on waste prevention and closed-loop resource recovery.

139

Sustainability

Meeting present human needs without compromising the ability of future generations to meet theirs.

140

Conservation

Protecting, preserving, and wisely managing natural resources and ecosystems for the future.

141

Preservation

Protecting natural areas and their features from harmful alteration or development.

142

Carbon Footprint

The total greenhouse gas emissions associated with an individual, product, organization, or event.

143

Climate Change

Long-term changes in Earth's atmospheric temperature, weather patterns, and sea levels.

144

Global Warming

The long-term rise in Earth's average surface temperature primarily driven by human greenhouse gas emissions.

145

Greenhouse Effect

A natural process in which greenhouse gases absorb and re-emit outgoing infrared radiation, helping warm Earth's surface and lower atmosphere.

146

Greenhouse Gases (GHGs)

Heat-trapping gases in the atmosphere, including carbon dioxide (CO₂), methane (CH₄), nitrous oxide (N₂O), and water vapor.

147

Chlorofluorocarbons (CFCs)

Synthetic industrial chemicals that destroy stratospheric ozone molecules.

148

Climate Mitigation

Actions aimed at reducing greenhouse gas emissions or increasing carbon removal from the atmosphere (e.g., renewable energy, reforestation).

149

Climate Adaptation

Adjustments made to reduce human and natural vulnerability to actual or expected climate impacts (e.g., flood barriers, drought-resistant crops).

150

Net Zero

A state, within a defined scope and period, in which greenhouse gas emissions are balanced by greenhouse gas removals.

151

Carbon Capture and Storage (CCS)

Technology that captures carbon dioxide (CO₂), often from industrial sources, and stores it in suitable geological formations.

152

Electric Vehicles (EVs)

Vehicles powered by electric motors and batteries; battery-electric vehicles have no tailpipe emissions while driving, but emissions may occur during electricity generation and manufacturing.

153

Heat Pump

Highly efficient technology that transfers heat into or out of buildings rather than creating heat through direct combustion.

154

Earth-Observation Satellites

Remote-sensing space technology used to monitor deforestation, wildfires, flooding, and ocean conditions across Earth.

155

Nature-Based Solutions

Strategies that protect, restore, or sustainably manage ecosystems to solve environmental and societal challenges (e.g., urban green spaces, wetland restoration).

156

Mangroves

Salt-tolerant coastal trees and shrubs that reduce shoreline erosion, trap sediment, provide fish nurseries, and store large amounts of carbon.

157

Mangrove Restoration

Re-establishing degraded mangrove forest ecosystems along coastal regions.

158

Wetlands

Areas where water covers or saturates soil permanently or seasonally (e.g., marshes, swamps, mangroves).

159

Coral Bleaching

The phenomenon where heat-stressed corals expel their microscopic symbiotic algae, turning white and risking death.

160

Urban Heat Island

Built-up urban areas becoming significantly warmer than surrounding rural areas due to concrete, asphalt, and sparse vegetation.

161

Sea Level Rise

An increase in average sea level caused in part by warming seawater expanding and by melting land-based glaciers and ice sheets.

162

Ocean Acidification

The ongoing decrease in ocean pH caused by oceans absorbing human-emitted carbon dioxide.

163

Habitat Fragmentation

Division of large continuous natural habitats into smaller, isolated patches due to human infrastructure.

164

Carbon Sequestration

Capturing and storing atmospheric carbon dioxide in natural sinks like forests, soils, and oceans.

165

Climate Resilience

The capability of natural or human systems to withstand, adapt to, and recover from climate disturbances.

166

Philippine Clean Air Act of 1999 (RA 8749)

Philippine law establishing a national air quality management program.

167

Ecological Solid Waste Management Act of 2000 (RA 9003)

Philippine law providing a systematic solid waste and recycling framework.

168

Philippine Clean Water Act of 2004 (RA 9275)

Philippine law protecting rivers, lakes, and coastal water bodies from land-based pollution.

169

Wildlife Resources Conservation and Protection Act (RA 9147)

Philippine law protecting wild flora, fauna, and their natural habitats.

170

National Integrated Protected Areas System (NIPAS) Act (RA 7586)

Philippine law establishing the NIPAS framework for protected areas; it was later amended by RA 11038.

171

Protected Area

A legally designated geographic space managed to achieve long-term nature conservation.

172

Department of Environment and Natural Resources (DENR)

Philippine government agency responsible for environmental and natural-resource matters.

173

Bureau of Fisheries and Aquatic Resources (BFAR)

Philippine agency responsible for fisheries and aquatic resources.

174

Philippine Atmospheric, Geophysical and Astronomical Services Administration (PAGASA)

Philippine state agency responsible for weather and climate services.

175

Philippine Institute of Volcanology and Seismology (PHIVOLCS)

Philippine agency monitoring volcanoes, earthquakes, and related hazards.

176

Organic Farming

A farming approach that follows organic standards and emphasizes ecological practices; rules for permitted inputs and genetically modified organisms depend on the applicable standard.

177

Rainwater Harvesting

Collecting and storing rainwater for later use.

178

Integrated Pest Management (IPM)

An approach that monitors and prevents pest problems and combines suitable control methods—such as biological, cultural, physical, and, when needed, chemical controls—while minimizing risks.

179

Ecological Footprint

A measure comparing human demand on nature with nature's capacity to regenerate resources and absorb wastes.

180

Carrying Capacity

The maximum population size of a species an ecosystem can sustain indefinitely.

181

Single-Use Plastics

Plastic products designed to be thrown away after one brief use (e.g., sachets, straws, plastic bags).

182

Protected Species

Organisms legally protected from hunting, logging, capture, or trade by conservation laws.

183

Conservation Biology

The scientific study focused on protecting biodiversity and preventing species extinction.

184

Environmental Impact Assessment (EIA)

A process for identifying and evaluating the potential environmental impacts of a proposed project to inform planning and decision-making.

185

Sustainable Development

Progress that satisfies current needs without compromising future generations.

186

Estuary

A coastal zone where freshwater from rivers mixes with saltwater from the ocean.

187

Intertidal Zone

Seashore area exposed to air at low tide and underwater at high tide.

188

Ecotone

A transition boundary zone between two distinct biomes or ecosystems.

189

Autotroph

An organism that synthesizes its own food from inorganic materials.

190

Heterotroph

An organism that obtains nutrition by consuming other living things.

191

Intraspecific Competition

Resource competition occurring between members of the same species.

192

Interspecific Competition

Resource competition occurring between members of different species.

193

Biogeochemical Cycle

Pathways by which essential chemical elements circulate through Earth's living and non-living systems.

194

Nitrogen Fixation

The conversion of atmospheric nitrogen gas (N₂) into biologically usable compounds, mainly by certain bacteria and archaea. Lightning can also convert nitrogen into compounds, but this is not biological nitrogen fixation.

195

Denitrification

Conversion of soil nitrates back into atmospheric nitrogen gas by soil bacteria.

196

Lithosphere

The solid, rigid outer crust and upper mantle of Earth.

197

Hydrosphere

All liquid, frozen, and gaseous water found across Earth.

198

Siltation

Water pollution where fine terrestrial soil particles settle onto aquatic beds, suffocating life.

199

Kaingin System

Slash-and-burn farming in the Philippines where forests are cleared and burned for farming, accelerating soil erosion.

200

Tamaraw

A critically endangered small dwarf buffalo endemic to the island of Mindoro, Philippines.

Additional Advanced Environmental Science Terms (201–250)

Part 1: Advanced Ecology & Population Dynamics

201

Autecology

The branch of ecology studying the interactions of an individual organism or a single species with its environment.

202

Synecology

The branch of ecology studying the structure and interactions of communities of multiple species in a shared area.

203

Limiting Factor

Any environmental condition (biotic or abiotic) that restricts the growth, abundance, or distribution of a population (e.g., nitrogen, sunlight).

204

Biotic Potential

The maximum reproductive capacity of a population under ideal environmental conditions with unlimited resources.

205

Environmental Resistance

The sum of all environmental factors (such as predators, drought, and disease) that prevent a population from reaching its biotic potential.

206

Liebig’s Law of the Minimum

The principle stating that growth is controlled not by total resources available, but by the scarcest resource (the limiting factor).

207

Shelford’s Law of Tolerance

The principle stating that an organism's survival depends on a range of conditions, having a specific minimum, maximum, and optimum range of tolerance for environmental factors.

208

Carrying Capacity (K)

The maximum population size of a species that an ecosystem can sustain indefinitely given available resources.

209

K-Selected Species

Species that invest heavily in a small number of offspring, exhibiting high parental care and long lifespans (e.g., elephants, humans).

210

r-Selected Species

Species that produce large numbers of offspring quickly with minimal parental investment and high offspring mortality (e.g., mosquitoes, frogs).

211

Ecotone

A transition boundary zone between two distinct ecological communities or biomes; it may contain high species richness due to the edge effect.

212

Keystone Species

A species that has a disproportionately large effect on its natural environment relative to its abundance (e.g., sea otters, sea stars).

213

Bioindicator

An organism or biological response used to assess the health and environmental quality of an ecosystem (e.g., lichens for air quality).

214

Phytoplankton

Microscopic photosynthetic organisms floating in aquatic environments that form the base of many aquatic food webs.

215

Zooplankton

Small, drifting or weakly swimming animals and larvae in aquatic ecosystems that consume phytoplankton or other particles.

Part 2: Aquatic Zones & Marine Chemistry

216

Photic Zone (Euphotic Zone)

The illuminated upper layer of a body of water where sufficient sunlight penetrates for photosynthesis to occur.

217

Aphotic Zone

The deep water layer where sunlight is insufficient for photosynthesis.

218

Benthic Zone

The ecological zone at the bottom of a body of water, including the sediment and subsurface layers.

219

Pelagic Zone

The open-water column of an ocean or lake, away from the bottom and shore.

220

Biochemical Oxygen Demand (BOD)

The amount of dissolved oxygen needed by aerobic microorganisms to break down organic matter in a water sample.

221

Dissolved Oxygen (DO)

The concentration of molecular oxygen dissolved in water, vital for fish and other aquatic organisms.

222

Thermal Stratification

The division of a lake or water body into distinct temperature layers, often including a warm upper epilimnion and a colder lower hypolimnion.

223

Halocline

A layer in a body of water where salinity changes rapidly with depth.

224

Pycnocline

A layer in an ocean or lake where water density increases rapidly with depth.

225

Thermocline

A layer in a large body of water where temperature changes more rapidly with depth than in the layers above or below.

Part 3: Atmospheric Science, Physics & Climate

226

Particulate Matter (PM₂.₅ and PM₁₀)

Fine suspended solid particles or liquid droplets in the air that can pose health risks when inhaled.

227

pH Scale

A logarithmic scale related to hydrogen-ion activity, commonly used to describe how acidic or basic a water or soil sample is.

228

Coriolis Effect

The apparent deflection of moving air and water caused by Earth's rotation; moving objects are deflected to the right in the Northern Hemisphere.

229

Intertropical Convergence Zone (ITCZ)

A low-pressure belt near the equator where trade winds converge, often producing clouds and heavy rainfall; its position influences rainfall in the Philippines.

230

Albedo Effect

The fraction of incoming solar energy reflected by a surface; light ice and snow generally have high albedo, while dark ocean water has low albedo.

231

Rain Shadow Effect

A relatively dry region on the leeward side of a mountain range, where descending air is often drier after moisture has been lost on the windward side.

232

Orographic Precipitation

Precipitation produced when moist air is forced to rise over elevated landforms such as mountains.

233

Aerosols

Tiny liquid or solid particles suspended in the atmosphere that can scatter or absorb sunlight and influence climate.

234

Adiabatic Cooling

Cooling that occurs when an air mass expands as pressure decreases, without exchanging heat with its surroundings.

235

Temperature Inversion

A condition in which temperature increases with height through a layer, which can limit vertical mixing and allow pollution to accumulate near the ground.

236

Urban Canopy Layer

The atmospheric layer extending from the ground to approximately the average roof height of buildings in an urban area.

237

Lapse Rate

The rate at which atmospheric temperature changes with altitude; in the troposphere, temperature generally decreases with height.

238

Radiative Forcing

A change in the balance between incoming energy from the Sun and outgoing energy from Earth, often expressed as a change in energy flux.

Part 4: Environmental Chemistry & Cycles

239

Anthropogenic

Originating from human activity; often used to describe environmental impacts, pollutants, or climate influences.

240

Xenobiotic

A chemical foreign to an organism or biological system, such as a synthetic pesticide.

241

Persistent Organic Pollutants (POPs)

Organic compounds that resist environmental degradation and may persist, travel long distances, and accumulate in organisms (e.g., DDT).

242

Carbonate Compensation Depth (CCD)

The ocean depth below which calcium carbonate dissolves faster than it is supplied, so carbonate sediments generally do not accumulate.

243

Eutrophic Lake

A lake with high nutrient concentrations and often abundant plant and algal growth; decomposition can cause low oxygen levels, especially at depth.

244

Oligotrophic Lake

A lake with low nutrient levels and relatively low plant and algal growth; such lakes are often clear and may have high dissolved oxygen in deep water.

245

Leaching

The process by which soluble nutrients or pollutants dissolve and are carried downward through soil by percolating water.

246

Methanogenesis

The biological production of methane (CH₄) by anaerobic microorganisms called methanogens in oxygen-deprived environments such as wetlands or landfills.

247

Sorption

A process in which a substance becomes attached to or taken up by another material; it includes adsorption and absorption.

248

Anoxia

A condition in which dissolved oxygen is absent or nearly absent in an aquatic environment; severe anoxia can harm or kill aquatic organisms.

249

Hypoxia

A condition of low dissolved oxygen in water; levels below about 2 mg/L are often used to describe severe aquatic hypoxia, though thresholds vary by species and setting.

250

Phytoremediation

The use of plants and their associated microbes to remove, contain, or break down pollutants in contaminated soil or water.
